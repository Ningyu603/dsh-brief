欧盟与英国完成 ETS 连接谈判

抓取时间：2026-11-23 06:09
正文字数：2738 字
原始来源：taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu

The conclusion of negotiations for an agreement to link the EU’s and UK’s respective Emission Trading Systems (ETS) announced on 20 November at the EU-UK summit paves the ground for a possible future mutual exemption from the partners’ respective Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms.

According to Regulation 2023/956, the EU CBAM applies to imports of goods under its scope regardless of their country of origin. However, CBAM does not apply to goods originating in third countries or territories, which are already part of the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) or have linked their own ETS to the EU ETS. These countries and territories are listed in Annex III of the EU CBAM Regulation.

The EU and UK committed to applying the ETS linking agreement as of 2028, subject to the ratification process and entry into force in both jurisdictions. On the EU side, Annex III of EU CBAM Regulation will need to be amended through a delegated act under Article 2(11) of the CBAM Regulation.

Once the EU-UK ETS linking agreement enters into force, and once the necessary legal steps are completed, the UK can be added to Annex III of the EU CBAM Regulation. The Commission stands ready to launch these procedures as soon as possible. This would mean that goods originating in the UK would be exempt from the EU CBAM. A corresponding approach is expected on the UK side under its own carbon border arrangements.

This agreement reflects the logic of the mechanism. The EU CBAM is designed to prevent carbon leakage by ensuring that imported goods face a carbon price equivalent to that paid by domestic producers under the ETS. Where two partners apply linked emissions trading systems, and therefore comparable carbon pricing rules, a border adjustment is no longer necessary. Exempting each other’s goods in these circumstances helps ensure the mechanism is fair, while avoiding double carbon charges and unnecessary administrative burden for businesses.

Linking the EU and the UK ETSs brings benefits for both sides. It will help ensure that the same set of climate rules related to emissions trading will apply in the EU and the UK, creating a level playing field for businesses on both sides of the Channel. It will also create a larger, more liquid carbon market with a more stable carbon price, supporting the EU and the UK on our paths to climate neutrality.

The mutual exemption from our respective CBAMs will also reduce red tape and costs for companies on both sides of the channel.
